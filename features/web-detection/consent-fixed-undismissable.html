The prep that makes painting easy

Weekend notes, filed in the spring edition

Painting a room goes faster when most of the effort is spent before the first coat. Fill the cracks, sand the patches smooth, wash the walls with sugar soap, and tape the edges carefully. Cut in around the ceiling and skirting with a brush, then roll the open areas in overlapping vertical strokes while the edges are still wet. Two thin coats always look better than one thick one.

Late summer is the best time to see the Milky Way from the northern hemisphere. Get away from street lights, give your eyes twenty minutes to adapt to the dark, and look south after midnight. The bright band runs from the horizon up through the Summer Triangle, three bright stars that are easy to pick out even from a town. A pair of ordinary binoculars reveals star clusters scattered along its length.

Meteor showers need no equipment at all, only patience and a comfortable chair. Lie back, look at a broad patch of sky about halfway up, and avoid staring at the radiant itself, since the longest trails appear some distance away from it. Expect quiet spells followed by bursts of several meteors in a minute. Dress warmly even in August; a clear night loses its heat quickly.

Feeding garden birds through the winter is simple, but a few habits make a difference. Clean the feeders every couple of weeks to stop disease spreading, offer fresh water as well as food, and place the feeders near cover so small birds can escape from hawks. Sunflower hearts suit most species, while a fat block draws in tits and woodpeckers on the coldest mornings.

The grind matters more than the beans. Coarse grounds suit a cafetiere and a four minute steep, medium grounds suit a filter cone, and fine grounds suit an espresso machine. If a filter brew tastes sour and thin, grind a little finer; if it tastes bitter and harsh, grind a little coarser. Weigh the coffee and water for a week, and you will soon learn to make the same good cup every morning.

New runners usually start too fast. A comfortable pace is one where you can hold a conversation, even if it feels slow. Alternate a few minutes of running with a minute of walking, and add a little more running each week. Rest days are part of the plan, not a break from it. After two months most people can run for half an hour without stopping, and they enjoy it far more.

The light in the hour after sunrise flatters almost any subject. Shadows are long and soft, colours are warm, and the streets are quiet. Walk the same route on several mornings and notice how the scene changes with the weather. Keep the horizon level, place the main subject away from the centre of the frame, and take one more picture after you think you are finished.

Knitting a first jumper is less daunting when it is broken into pieces. Swatch first, wash and dry the swatch, and measure it honestly; the yarn label is only a starting point. Knit the back, then the front, then the sleeves, and block each piece to size before sewing them together. The seams hold the shape of the garment, so take your time with them and use the same yarn.

Paddling on a calm lake at dawn is one of the simplest pleasures of summer. Check the forecast for wind, wear a buoyancy aid even if you swim well, and tell someone when you plan to be back. Keep close to the shore on your first outings and practise turning and stopping before heading across open water. Herons and kingfishers are easiest to see before the boats arrive.

Most houseplants die from kindness rather than neglect. Water only when the top few centimetres of compost are dry, empty the saucer afterwards, and let the plant rest through the darker months with less water and no feed. Turn the pot a quarter turn each week so the stems grow straight, and wipe the dust off broad leaves so they can make the most of the winter light.

Tomatoes reward patience more than any other summer crop. Start the seeds indoors six to eight weeks before the last frost, keep the trays somewhere bright, and resist the urge to overwater. When the seedlings have two sets of true leaves, move them into deeper pots and bury the stems up to the first leaves; the buried section will sprout roots and give the plant a sturdier base for the months ahead.
